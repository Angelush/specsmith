# 29% Of Your Employees Are Sabotaging Your AI Rollout. The Fix Is 3 Things.

A survey found roughly a third of employees globally admit to sabotaging AI rollouts. Nate walks through the three principles he uses with leaders to head that off: (1) an explicit, public leadership commitment that names what the rollout is not designed to do (cut current headcount), borrowing Jensen Huang's "productivity enhancer, not a job cutter" framing; (2) scoping the first rollout to one bottom-line-tied, already-proven use case with a passionate mid-level champion, and defining success as a business outcome rather than usage volume (Uber's public regret over the token spend it had encouraged is the cautionary tale); and (3) getting an honest, ground-truth verdict on pilot value before scaling — diagnosing specific failure causes instead of blindly persisting or blindly quitting — then sequencing the wider rollout message as pilot proof, then business vision, then safeguards, then what stays human. He closes on the idea that AI's biggest job impact is blurring role boundaries rather than eliminating roles, which is what actually drives the ambiguity and resistance.
