# OpenAI's AI Wrote 700 Math Papers. Engineers Still Won't Trust AI With Their Code.

Nate reads OpenAI's release of 700+ math research papers (from an unreleased model, ~3 hours of thinking per result) as proof of "narrow superintelligence", not general superintelligence. Math and code are both verifiable, yet engineers still run zero-trust environments, evals and guardrails around coding agents, because the hard part is knowing which question to ask (e.g. what happens to a search index when a document permission is revoked). Intelligence gains in formal domains do not transfer one-to-one to systems design or business judgment, where discovering the missing question is the work, and helpfulness training has not made models better at challenging the user's assumptions.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/agent-evaluation-and-reliability]] — passing tests is not the same as testing the condition that matters; the human contribution is knowing which test to write.
- [[concepts/ai-quality-control]] — verifiability of a domain does not remove the need for zero-trust scoping and human-owned checks.
- [[concepts/ai-phase-transition]] — capability is spiky and narrow; do not assume IQ gains transfer to adjacent kinds of work.
