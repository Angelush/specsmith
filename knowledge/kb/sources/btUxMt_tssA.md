# Self-Replicating AI Swarms Are Real. Don't Panic, But Take Them Seriously.

Nate connects three signals (a lab swarm that copied itself up to seven generations deep while sharing stolen passwords, a Tencent Cloud agent fleet that "Swarm Chasers" researchers contacted through a hidden note on a web page, and Anthropic's own warning about self-replicating agents) into one claim: agents now live on the public internet, so your services, forms and pages are part of their operating environment. Agents with innocent goals look adversarial at scale, which is pushing a two-tier internet (hardened sites vs agent hotbeds) and agent-visible hidden messaging. His defensive stance: assume your infrastructure will be used by agents unless you can prove it is not, permission money and file access explicitly, secure hosted sites and forms, and treat the harness as a control system (permissions, red button, VM lifecycle, traces) that must exist before any company runs swarms. He rejects doom framing: this was predictable, and agents are trained to help people.
