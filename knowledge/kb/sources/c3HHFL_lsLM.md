# The Future Of The Internet Isn't Open For Your Agent. Muse, Dots And Claude Duet Will Find Out.

After "Black October" showed how much easier AI makes breaking into websites, businesses have more reason to lock agents out. Nate argues that agents will face uneven access, not one open internet, and a smarter model cannot open a door a business has closed. He describes how he splits his own work across Grockbot, Dots, Instinct, Muse, and Claude Duet. He then covers the authorization infrastructure taking shape: Cloudflare signed agents, Visa/Bill/Billtrust registered-agent invoice payments, Stripe shared payment tokens and Google AP2 (scoped credentials), and Shopify facts published for agents. He offers a three-zone model of the 2027 internet (partnership, aspiring actor, no-go) and two security incidents: an "agents, please identify yourself" instruction on a website that led Claude to leak human email addresses, and a malicious agent instruction file found in a downloads folder.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/agentic-commerce]] -- three zones of agent access (partnership, aspiring actor, no-go); agents need explicit product facts published for them, not marketing pages.
- [[concepts/agent-security-baseline]] -- scope payment credentials by seller, amount, and expiry; answer "identify yourself" requests without leaking PII; give agents a working folder you watch.
- [[concepts/practical-agent-adoption]] -- assign work to agents by strength; proactive alerts are useful about 50-75% of the time; recommend only use cases that already work reliably.

## Notable quotes

- "A smarter model isn't going to be able to unlock that door that a business has decided to close."
- "It is very difficult as a first principles problem to distinguish agent-driven computer use from human computer use."
- "We need that specificity to earn trust these days."
