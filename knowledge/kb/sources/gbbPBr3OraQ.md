# I Gave Meta's Muse The Most Boring Job I Had. It Found $5,350 A Year.

Nate hands Meta's consumer assistant Muse (launched Sept 8, number one iPhone app within two weeks) a boring admin job: audit his subscriptions. It found $5,350 a year and had cancelled $1,285 so far. He uses the product to argue that once capability is table stakes, the winner is the assistant that knows your circumstances and turns context into judgment, and that an assistant sitting between you and sellers moves the commercial fight (Amazon blocking Muse, Walmart and Shopify joining) from attention and ads to who owns the customer relationship. He also praises Muse's UX for non-technical users: a named avatar, emoji status pills, phone-number-only onboarding.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/agentic-commerce]] — an assistant that decides what to buy moves value from ad-funded attention to the agent; Amazon's block is a platform fight, not a paperwork dispute.
- [[concepts/agent-memory-systems]] — personal context only matters when translated into judgment (duplicate vs. keep, waste vs. twice-a-year need); ask when unsure.
- [[concepts/practical-agent-adoption]] — make proposed, in-progress and completed actions legible without reading; consumer agents win on ordinary, boring jobs.

## Notable quotes

- "AI needs to not feel like AI to deliver AI value."
- "A very capable model that starts every conversation without that information is still going to make you do a tremendous amount of work."
