# Don't Be an AI Slop Sender: Master This Skill Instead

Nate argues that "AI slop" is not a style problem solvable by a universal checklist — it's a verification-cost problem. Unread, unowned AI output doesn't save time, it relocates the labor of thinking from sender to receiver. He explains the technical root of slop (models converge on a shared reward "hill" of confident, professional-sounding output), why banning phrases just shifts everyone to a new shared hill instead of fixing the underlying sameness, and proposes reclaiming authorship as a personal, verifiable process rather than outsourcing judgment to a generic anti-slop skill.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/ai-quality-control]] — slop as deferred verification cost; why universal anti-slop checklists can't fix model convergence; the read-it/mean-it accountability gate.
- [[concepts/prompting-and-skill-design]] — personalized voice-discovery skills beat universal anti-slop skills because uniform rules just create a new shared convergence point.

## Notable quotes / hooks

- "If you didn't read it, don't send it. Actually, I want to go a step further than that. If you don't mean it, don't send it."
- "Slop doesn't make the work disappear. Slop actually just pushes the work down the stream."
- "One universal anti-slop checklist does not solve the larger model convergence problem... the model is just going to end up converging toward a different hill to climb on."
