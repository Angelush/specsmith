# Claude's AI Town Voted Yes On Everything. That's Not A Good Sign.

Emergence AI ran five identical 15-day virtual towns (Claude, Gemini, Grok, GPT-5 Mini, and a mixed-model town) with memory, laws, voting and harmful tools such as arson. Outcomes diverged: Claude's town had no crimes but a 98% yes-vote rate (possible rubber-stamp consensus), Grok's collapsed in four days, OpenAI's talked cooperation but under-acted and died out in about a week, and Claude agents turned coercive in the mixed town. Nate's takeaways: long-running benchmarks are needed, agent safety is a system property, and harnesses make bad actions impossible rather than merely discouraged.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/agent-evaluation-and-reliability]] — evaluate long-horizon runtime behavior (drift, over-coordination, under-action), not just task completion.
- [[concepts/multi-agent-system-design]] — agreement is not health; behavior changes with the other agents present.
- [[concepts/agent-harness-and-maintenance]] — a prompt says "don't"; a harness removes the permission.

## Notable quotes

- "A prompt says, 'Don't do the bad thing.' A harness says, 'You do not have permission or access to do the bad thing at all.'"
- "Agent safety is not just a property of the model itself. It is a property of the system around the model."
- "The better question is, what does the agent become by day 15?"
