# productivity draft

(Working title from the playlist; the video is about "productivity theater" with agents.) Nate argues that agent swarms, factories and parallel agents make it harder than ever to tell productivity theater from real output, and that agents demand *more* intent from the human, not less. He gives four principles drawn from how he manages agents in Claude and Codex: (1) count the human work that comes after the agent finishes, (2) run more experiments than you commit to, (3) decide when an assignment ends before making it recurring, and (4) give the managing "chief of staff" agent a budget for your attention. He closes with a three-question audit of running agents and a "truth-teller" prompt (on Substack) that gets agents to admit when something does not matter.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/ai-productivity-workflows]] — productivity theater; value output by downstream human cost, not by how hard it was to make without AI; three-question audit of running agents.
- [[concepts/knowledge-work-delegation]] — assign the check, not the polish: "show your work" and verifier-agent assignments aimed at the step the human must trust.
- [[concepts/multi-agent-system-design]] — parallel agents should diversify understanding of one problem, not produce competing solutions; know when the missing evidence is outside agent reach.
- [[concepts/agent-harness-and-maintenance]] — recurring agents outlive their reason; set stop conditions before making anything recurring.
- [[concepts/ai-personal-stack]] — chief-of-staff agent with an attention budget: a human-friendly, not agent-friendly, structure of engagement.
