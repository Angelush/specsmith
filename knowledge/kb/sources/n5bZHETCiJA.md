# The Race to Done: Fable 5.1 vs GPT-6 Astra. Who Wins?

Nate gave Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra the same five-line brief (a native Mac clipboard manager) and got two different, working apps (Ledge and Shelf). The real comparison is the second and third round: Astra's speed allowed three versions in the time Fable built one, surfacing preferences he could not have put in the first prompt. He lays out a role-fit view of models (Fable to think a problem through, Astra to structure, draft and use the computer fast), and argues a single agent with a short prompt got real software built without multi-agent machinery.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/prompting-and-skill-design]] — a short brief plus fast iteration reveals undecided preferences; running two models on one brief maps the solution space.
- [[concepts/model-comparison-and-performance]] — compare models by role fit per task phase, not a single winner; iteration speed changes outcome quality.
- [[concepts/multi-agent-system-design]] — single agent first; open multi-agent setups assign roles (design vs check vs cheap execution) over a shared repo and message board.

## Notable quotes

- "You discover that you hadn't decided exactly what you wanted, nor could you with a five-line prompt."
- "It's my job to figure out where that intelligence needs to be applied and put that model in the right spot."
