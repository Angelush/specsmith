# GPT-6 Astra Doesn't Need Your Instructions Anymore.

Nate argues GPT-6 Astra is the first "super agent": given years of email, calendar and writing and left alone for days, it chose its own method, tooling and environment. He frames this as a post-prompt world where you hand over standing areas of concern rather than task-shaped asks, where work that leaves a checkable trace moves to agents first, and where the bottleneck becomes a trust curve (closing the last 1-2 percent) and human ownership of decisions touching time, money and relationships. He closes with questions to settle before giving an agent a standing job.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/knowledge-work-delegation]] — delegate standing jobs with no natural end; settle scope, memory, promises and oversight before handing over.
- [[concepts/agent-evaluation-and-reliability]] — work with a checkable doorway (tests, screens, tie-out numbers) moves first; reliability is a trust curve, not an intelligence curve.
- [[concepts/agent-memory-systems]] — an agent that has worked with you for months beats a fresh copy of the same model; tribal knowledge risk reappears as machine knowledge.

## Notable quotes

- "We can give away ongoing areas of concern."
- "Code leaves tests behind. A website leaves a screen that an agent can inspect."
- "A $10 decision is not the same as a buy-a-car decision."
