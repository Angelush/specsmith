# Cheap software made your PM job harder, not easier. Here's the new job.

Nate argues the "PMs should prototype" advice is oversold and misses the real shift: AI makes generation easier, which moves the bottleneck from *can we build this* to *what deserves to exist*. He uses Microsoft's internal Power Platform footprint (1M+ citizen-dev assets — 18,000+ agent environments, 170,000 Power Apps, 50,000 Power Automate flows, 1,200 chatbots) to argue the new PM job is classifying software abundance — personal tool, team beta, supported internal product, customer-facing product — with governance framed as enabling builders rather than gatekeeping them, and demotion treated as seriously as promotion so the company doesn't accumulate a junk drawer of unowned software as its new tech debt.

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/organizational-ai-transformation]] — the PM job shifts from rationing scarce engineering time to classifying software abundance via a four-rung production class ladder (personal tool -> team beta -> supported internal product -> customer-facing product), with an "open discovery" posture (ask what was built and what it touches, don't just gatekeep) for the messy "prototype commons" that forms before anything gets classified.
- [[concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption]] — Microsoft's 1M+-asset internal Power Platform ecosystem as a governance-as-enablement case study (inventory, telemetry, permission review, environment controls, data policy), paired with GitGuardian's 2026 stat that AI service secrets exposed on public GitHub hit 1.2M in 2025 (+81% YoY) as the risk case for why that governance can't be skipped.

## Notable quotes

- "AI makes generation easier. So, it moves the bottleneck."
- "The scarcest thing is great judgment about what ought to exist, what ought to be deleted, who the product is for."
- "A ladder that only moves upward is going to become a junk drawer... that is the new tech debt."
