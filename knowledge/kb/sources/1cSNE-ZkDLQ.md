# You Can't Compete on Cheap Models Anymore

Uses Mitchell Hashimoto's (HashiCorp co-founder) two-part experiment with Fable 5 — a mundane feature task where a sub-$1 model tied a $9 frontier model, versus a $40, 2-hour frontier-model job optimizing his own systems code that no cheap model could touch and that he couldn't have hit himself — to argue that AI execution is commoditizing fast, and the real, durable multiplier on AI's value is human imagination: the size of your list of things you know how to ask a model for. Extends this from individuals to organizations via Stripe's one-day 50-million-line migration and a factory-electrification analogy (redesign the building, not just bolt on the new motor).

## Concepts covered

- [[concepts/ai-builder-mindset]] — imagination, not model cost, sets the AI value multiplier; cheap models converge on known tasks, frontier value only appears on tasks nobody has imagined asking for yet; imagination is built via hands-on "scouting hours," not benchmark-reading.
- [[concepts/model-selection-frameworks]] — run a two-layer stack: cheap/open models handle daily known execution aggressively, frontier models get a surgical, targeted "scouting" role on the questions that redefine the task list itself.
- [[concepts/organizational-ai-transformation]] — you can't hire your way out of an imagination shortage because imagination only fires next to context; the fix is giving context-holders access and permission to pose expensive frontier-model questions, and building the organizational infrastructure (review systems, test coverage, team habits) that lets frontier wins actually land.

## Notable quotes / hooks

- "AI can only do work that someone has imagined. These cheap coding tools, they execute, but they don't decide what's worth executing."
- "It's the size of your list of things you know how to ask for. It's your imagination."
- "You can't imagine with capabilities you haven't touched. Nobody imagines a use for a tool they've read a summary of."
- "The job isn't hiring imagination. It's manufacturing it."
